# One Core module owns the day change, and the shell only reports the foreground

From 0.4 a new local day changes several Core features at once: today becomes an Active Day, the Word of the Day module takes the Word of the day's morning notification (ADR 0016's amendment for 0.4), the notification plan is made again (ADR 0021) and the Feed opens the day with its Word of the Day. Until now each feature noticed the day on its own, and the trigger, a timer to the next local midnight and the app's return to the foreground, lived in an untested React module that called the composition. A notification tapped from the background reached the composition twice at once, through the app's return and through the Word it opened, and both took the morning Word and wrote the Active Day side by side.

The day change is one module inside the composition (ADR 0018). It alone decides that a new local day started, in the clock's time zone, and it turns the composition to it in one fixed order: the morning Word becomes the Word of the Day, today an Active Day, the plan is made again and the Feed opens the day. The morning Word comes first, so nothing reads the day or drops a passed morning record before it is taken. Every report runs in one turn, so the app's return and a notification's Word are ordered and either order ends the same. A Word opened from a notification keeps today's Active Day in its own write, with its Seen mark and History entry, and its card is given once that write is kept; the rest of the turn goes on behind it. A report on the same day only records the Active Day again, which writes nothing once it is kept, and the app's return reads the permission again and makes the plan by it.

The shell keeps a lifecycle adapter: it tells the composition the app is in the foreground when the app comes back and on a timer set to the next local day, and it holds no day rule. The Feed no longer reads the clock to find a new day: the composition tells it the day it turned to, and the Feed opens a day it hasn't opened with its Word of the Day yet at the first report of the card on screen that day, as before this module, or at once when that report came before the composition turned to the day, as on the app's return. With the app open over midnight, the card got ready the day before so stays, and the Word of the Day comes after the reader's next swipe. A plan made before the day's first report, such as across a time-zone change with the app open, asks the day change first, which takes the morning Word of a day it hasn't turned to; the plan itself no longer takes it.

## Considered Options

- **Each feature notices the new day itself**, as before: rejected. The rule lived in six places, the trigger had no test, and two reports at once ran side by side.
- **The Core sets the midnight timer itself**: rejected. Timers stop while the app is in the background, so the shell needs the foreground event anyway. The shell's timer takes the clock, so a test with a fake clock drives it, and the Core decides the day from the clock it is given.
- **Record the Active Day first, also for a notification's Word**: rejected. The opened Word's Seen mark, its History entry and today's Active Day are kept in one write (spec 0.4), so the Active Day follows the morning Word on every path.
- **The Feed opens the day as soon as the composition turns to it**: rejected. With the app open over midnight, the timer's report would put the Word of the Day in place of the card got ready the day before, which the reader would otherwise reach with the next swipe, and the day change is not meant to change what the reader sees.
- **Give the opened Word's card once the whole turn is done**: rejected. The tap would wait for the device's scheduler and the Feed, and a failure of either would leave the Entry screen empty although the Word is kept.
